Golden nugget

Do this before anything else

Here is the single most impactful thing you can do right now, before you write a single prompt. Set up custom instructions. This tells your AI tool about you, your business, and how you want it to write. Every conversation after this will be better because the AI already knows your context.

How to set it up

1

ChatGPT: Click your profile picture → Settings → Personalisation → Custom instructions

2

Claude: Click your profile picture → Settings → Profile → fill in the "About you" and "What would you like Claude to know?" sections

3

Gemini: Go to Settings → Extensions → fill in your preferences

What to paste in

Copy the template below and customise the parts in [brackets]. This is the exact system we use. It stops AI writing like a robot and makes it write like you.

// Your custom instructions template

[Your name]. [Your job title] at [your business name].

[One sentence about what your business does and who you serve].

Based in [your location]. Audience is [describe your customers].

I primarily use AI for [list 2-3 main use cases, e.g. "writing emails, creating social media content, and summarising meeting notes"].

SHOULD use clear, direct language.

SHOULD keep sentences short and sharp.

SHOULD write in active voice.

SHOULD give practical, specific advice.

SHOULD use bullet point lists for social media posts.

SHOULD include data, numbers, or concrete examples when possible.

SHOULD speak to the reader using "you" and "your."

AVOID em dashes. Use only commas, periods, or semicolons.

AVOID filler phrases that connect ideas too loosely.

AVOID constructions like "not just X, but Y."

AVOID metaphors, analogies, and cliches.

AVOID vague or sweeping claims.

AVOID phrases like "in conclusion," "to sum up," or "closing."

AVOID extra adjectives or adverbs.

AVOID hashtags, markdown, or asterisks.

OUTPUT should read clean, concise, and how a human naturally writes.

IMPORTANT: Review your response and ensure no em dashes!

Why this works so well

Without custom instructions, AI defaults to its "house style": wordy, full of em dashes, and stuffed with words like "leverage" and "synergy." It sounds like a corporate brochure.

With these rules in place, every response sounds like you wrote it. You will spend less time editing, and people will not be able to tell AI helped. That is the goal.

Test it: After pasting in your custom instructions, ask AI to "Write me a LinkedIn post about [something relevant to your business]." Compare the result with and without custom instructions. The difference is night and day.

Your first prompts

The skill that makes AI actually useful

The difference between a bad AI response and a brilliant one is how you ask. Here is the formula that makes everything work better.

The RCTC framework

R

Role

Tell AI who to be

"You are a marketing expert who specialises in small business social media"

C

Context

Give background

"I run a plumbing business in Newcastle with 3 employees"

T

Task

Be specific about what you want

"Write me 5 Instagram post ideas for this week"

C

Constraints

Set limits and rules

"Keep each caption under 50 words, use UK English, no emojis"

5 hands-on exercises to do right now

Do not just read these. Open your AI tool and actually do them. That is how you learn.

1

Rewrite a real email

Open your sent folder. Find an email you spent too long writing. Paste it into your AI tool with this prompt:

Rewrite this email to be clearer and more professional. Keep my key points but make it half the length.

[Paste your email here]

Compare the two versions. What is better? What would you tweak?

Summarise a document

Find a long document, report, or article you need to read. Paste it into your AI tool with this prompt:

[Paste the document here]

Try NotebookLM for this. Upload the document and chat with it. You can ask follow-up questions, and it will always answer based on what your document actually says.

3

Draft a social media post

Use the RCTC framework to write a post about your business. Be specific about your business, audience, and platform. Here is an example to adapt:

Role: You are a social media expert who writes engaging posts for small businesses.

Context: I run a [your business type] in [your location]. My audience is [describe your ideal customer].

Task: Write me a [platform] post about [topic]. Include a caption and 5 relevant hashtags.

Constraints: Keep the caption under 100 words. Use a friendly, professional tone. UK English.

Template 1

Weekly email template generator

You are a professional communication specialist. I need to send a weekly update email to [audience, e.g. "my team of 8 people"]. The email should cover: what we achieved this week, priorities for next week, and any blockers or things people need to know. Write this week's email based on the following notes:

Keep it under 200 words. Friendly but professional tone. UK English. Use bullet points for the key updates.

Use in: ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini. Customise: swap in your audience, adjust the tone, add your company's sign-off style.

Template 2

Social media post batch creator

You are a social media strategist for small businesses. I run a [business type] in [location]. My target audience is [describe your ideal customer]. Create 5 social media posts for this week. For each post include: the platform (Instagram, LinkedIn, or Facebook), a caption (under 80 words), 5 relevant hashtags, and a suggested content type (image, video, carousel, or text). Mix up the content types. Use a friendly, authentic tone. UK English.

Use in: ChatGPT or Claude. Customise: add your brand voice guidelines, specify topics you want covered.

Template 3

Meeting notes summariser

You are an executive assistant who excels at meeting summaries. Here are my raw notes from a meeting. Summarise them into a clear format with these sections: Key decisions made, Action items (include who is responsible and the deadline), Open questions still to resolve, and Next steps. Keep it concise and scannable. Use bullet points throughout.

[Paste your meeting notes here]

Use in: ChatGPT, Claude, or NotebookLM. Customise: add the names of attendees, include your project context.

Template 4

Customer FAQ responder

You are a customer service specialist for [your business type]. Here are the 10 questions our customers ask most often:

[List your FAQs here]

Write a clear, helpful answer for each question. Keep each answer under 50 words. Use a warm, reassuring tone. These will be used in email replies and on our website. UK English.

Power features you should know

Get more from the tools you already have

Most people use about 10% of what AI tools can do. Here are the features that will genuinely change how you work. All of these are available on the free tiers of their respective tools.

Project folders (ChatGPT and Claude)

Stop losing your best conversations. Both ChatGPT and Claude let you create project folders to organise chats by topic, client, or project. This means:

All your marketing chats in one place
Separate folders for each client or project
Easy to find previous conversations without scrolling
In Claude, you can add project-specific instructions so the AI has context for that folder every time

Set this up now: Create three folders, one for your most common use case (e.g. "Social media"), one for a current project, and one called "Experiments" for testing new things.

Canvas mode (ChatGPT)

Canvas opens a split-screen editor next to your chat. Instead of AI dumping a wall of text and you copying it somewhere else, you can edit the document right there while chatting with AI about changes.

Best use case

Writing anything longer than a quick email. Blog posts, proposals, reports, policies. You can highlight a specific paragraph and say "make this section more persuasive" without affecting the rest of the document. Much more efficient than re-generating the whole thing.

To use it: start a new chat in ChatGPT and select "Canvas" from the model dropdown, or ask ChatGPT to "open this in canvas."

Deep research mode (ChatGPT and Gemini)

This is like having a research assistant who reads the entire internet for you. Deep research browses dozens of sources, cross-references information, and gives you a proper report with citations. It takes a few minutes to run, but the results are far better than a normal search.

Best use case

Competitor analysis, market research, understanding a new topic in depth, or finding specific data you need for a business decision. Ask something like: "Research the top 10 CRM tools for small businesses in the UK. Compare pricing, key features, and which is best for a team of under 10 people."

In ChatGPT: select "Deep research" from the model dropdown. In Gemini: select "Deep Research" at the top of a new chat.

Web search tips

ChatGPT and Claude can both search the web, but they work differently to Google. Here is how to get better results:

Tip What to do Example
Be specific Give detail about your situation, not generic keywords "What is the best accounting software for a UK sole trader with under 50 invoices per month?"
Ask for sources Forces the AI to back up its claims with real references "Include links to sources for each recommendation"
Set a date Avoid outdated results by specifying a timeframe "Only include information from 2025 onwards"
Cross-check For anything important, verify using Perplexity or the original source AI search is good but not infallible. Trust, then verify.
