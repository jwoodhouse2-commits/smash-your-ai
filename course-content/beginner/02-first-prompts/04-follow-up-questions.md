Most people ask AI a question, glance at the answer, and either accept it or give up. That's leaving half the value on the table.

The real superpower of chat AI is that you can **keep talking to it**. Like a colleague you're collaborating with, not a vending machine.

## The follow-up is where the magic happens

Here's the thing. A first answer is based on the AI's best guess of what you want. A follow-up is based on **what you actually said about the first answer**. That's ten times more information.

Watch:

**Round 1:**

> Write a LinkedIn post announcing our new service.

You get a generic post. Fine, but bland.

**Round 2:**

> This is close but it sounds like every other LinkedIn post. Rewrite it so it opens with a contrarian statement people will pause on. Keep the same overall structure.

Now you've got something that stops the scroll.

**Round 3:**

> The opening line is great. The middle section is too long. Cut it by half. Keep the opening and the sign-off exactly as they are.

Now you've got something worth publishing.

Three rounds. Maybe two minutes. The result is miles better than any one-shot prompt could produce.

## Five follow-ups worth memorising

Copy these. Use them as a starter kit.

### 1. "Make it half as long."

Works on almost any AI answer. LLMs default to verbose. You usually want crisper.

### 2. "Rewrite this in my voice."

Paste two or three paragraphs you've actually written (emails, posts, anything) and say *"rewrite this in the voice of the examples above"*. Your tone, much more reliably.

### 3. "What's the weakest part of this answer and how would you fix it?"

Forces the AI to self-critique. You'll often get a better redraft just from asking.

### 4. "Give me three more options, each with a different angle."

Stops you getting anchored on the first answer. Variety breaks you out of the default.

### 5. "What am I not asking that I should be?"

The golden question. Especially useful after a business, legal, or planning question. The AI will often surface things you'd have missed.

## The rule

If you're about to accept an AI answer and you have a slight nagging feeling about it, **describe the feeling to the AI**. That's the follow-up.

*"This sounds too corporate."* *"The ending lands flat."* *"It repeats itself in the middle."* *"I don't believe the third point."*

You don't need to know *how* to fix it. The AI does. Just tell it what's wrong.
