Most small business owners lose hours a week to admin writing: chasing emails, drafting follow-ups, summarising calls, writing meeting agendas. This lesson gives you five copy-and-paste prompts to hand that work off to AI.

For each, paste the prompt into ChatGPT, Gemini, or Claude. Replace the [brackets] with your own details.

## Prompt 1: The "chase this up" email

> You are a polite but direct assistant. Draft a follow-up email to [person's name] at [company]. Two weeks ago we [what was agreed or sent]. I haven't heard back. I don't want to sound annoyed. I want to give them an easy out but also make it clear I'm ready to move forward. Keep it under 120 words. Sign off from [my name].

**Why it works:** Specifies the tone (*"polite but direct"*), acknowledges the awkwardness (*"don't want to sound annoyed"*), and caps the length.

## Prompt 2: Meeting agenda from a mess of notes

> You are a chief of staff. Below is a dump of thoughts about a meeting with [team/client] on [date]. Turn it into a clear agenda with 3-5 items, each with a time estimate (total: [e.g. 45 min]) and a named owner. End with a line about desired outcomes.
>
> Notes:
>
> [paste your rambling notes here]

**Why it works:** The AI loves tidy structured output. Dump in chaos, get back a proper agenda.

## Prompt 3: The meeting recap you send after a call

> You are taking notes on my behalf. I just had a call with [person] about [topic]. Below is my rough transcript / scribbled notes. Turn it into:
>
> 1. A one-paragraph summary of what we agreed.
> 2. A bulleted list of action items, each with an owner (me or them) and a deadline where one was mentioned.
> 3. Three questions I should have asked but didn't.
>
> Notes:
>
> [paste]

**Why it works:** The "three questions I should have asked" is the underrated bit. It makes the AI flag gaps you didn't spot.

## Prompt 4: The difficult message

> You are a communications coach. I need to [fire a supplier / decline a request / deliver critical feedback / chase a late payment]. Here's the situation: [describe briefly]. Draft the message. It should be respectful, firm, and direct. No waffle, no over-apology, no "I hope you're well". Under 150 words. Give me 2 versions: one slightly warmer, one slightly cooler.

**Why it works:** Gives you two drafts so you can mix and match the bits that feel right.

## Prompt 5: The proposal or quote email

> Write a proposal email to [client name] for [project / service]. Our company is [brief description, size, specialism]. The project is [description]. Scope includes: [list]. Timeline: [duration]. Price: [amount]. Our tone is [warm / professional / expert / etc.]. The email should cover the opener, the proposal, next steps, and a friendly sign-off. Around 200 words. At the end, list 3 questions I should make sure I've answered in my own head before sending this.

**Why it works:** The "3 questions" trick at the end surfaces anything you'd have missed.

## The routine

The time saving is real, but it's not automatic. Set yourself up properly:

1. **Bookmark the prompts** in your notes app or a text expander.
2. **Keep a "context block"** about your business that you paste at the top of every admin prompt. Saves retyping it.
3. **Don't send the first draft.** Tweak for your voice, check for hallucinations, then hit send.
