Every time you craft a really good prompt, one of two things happens.

1. You copy it somewhere and use it again in six months.
2. You lose it in the chat history and spend 10 minutes remaking it next time.

Most people are stuck in option 2. This lesson gets you to option 1.

## The simplest possible system

Open your notes app. Make a note called **"AI Prompt Library"**. Add this structure:

```
## Admin
[prompts for emails, meetings, chasing]

## Writing
[prompts for posts, blog drafts, captions]

## Research
[prompts for summarising, comparing, finding gaps]

## Personal
[prompts you've made for yourself specifically]
```

Whenever you write a prompt that works, **paste it in** under the right heading. Give it a one-line name so you can find it later.

That's it. No app. No subscription. No Airtable base. Just a note file.

## What to actually save

Not every prompt is worth saving. Save the ones that meet two tests.

1. **Did it save you time today?**
2. **Will you want to do this task again in the next six months?**

If yes to both, save it. Otherwise let it go.

## A few tips for future-you

When you save a prompt, tidy it up first. Take 20 seconds to:

- **Replace specifics with [brackets]** so you can swap them next time. *"Write a follow-up email to [client name]"* not *"Write a follow-up email to Sarah at Acme"*.
- **Add a one-line note** at the top saying when it's useful. *"For chasing up quotes after two weeks of silence."*
- **Note which AI you used.** Sometimes a prompt works beautifully in Claude and flops in ChatGPT. Good to know.
