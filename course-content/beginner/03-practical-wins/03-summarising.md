Most people never use AI for summarising because they don't realise it can read the whole document for them. It can. This lesson shows how.

## What can you feed in

Modern AI tools handle a surprising amount.

- **PDFs.** ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini all accept PDF uploads.
- **Word docs.** Same.
- **Spreadsheets.** Uploading works; the AI will often describe the data rather than truly analyse, but for a summary that's fine.
- **Web pages.** Paste the URL into Gemini, or ask ChatGPT to "summarise this link".
- **YouTube videos.** Paste the URL and ask for a summary. Works with Gemini especially well.
- **Meeting transcripts.** Record with any tool (Otter, Fathom, Zoom's built-in), export, paste.

There are limits: very long files (hundreds of pages) sometimes need chunking. But for most real business documents, upload-and-ask just works.

## The three summary prompts

Different situations need different summaries. These three cover 90% of cases.

### 1. The "what's in here" summary

> Summarise the attached document in:
>
> - A one-sentence verdict.
> - A 3-bullet "main points" list.
> - A 2-sentence note on what's missing or weak.

Good for: deciding whether the document is even worth reading in full.

### 2. The "make me look prepared" summary

> I need to walk into a meeting in 10 minutes having read this document. Give me:
>
> - 3 things the document argues.
> - 3 questions I could ask that would make me look like I'd read it carefully.
> - 1 thing I should push back on if asked.

Good for: meeting prep when you didn't have time to read the prework.

### 3. The "extract the actions" summary

> This is a meeting transcript / email thread. Give me:
>
> - A one-paragraph summary of what was discussed.
> - A table of action items with columns: who, what, by when.
> - Anything that was decided but not explicitly written down.

Good for: the morning after a long meeting.

## A critical habit

Always do this: after the AI's summary, ask **"what did you leave out that I'd regret missing?"**

It's a surprisingly good prompt. The AI will often flag a key point, concern, or nuance that didn't fit the summary but should be on your radar.

## Where it goes wrong

Three ways summaries let you down, all avoidable.

1. **Numbers or quotes are slightly off.** If the summary includes a specific statistic or quote, check it against the source. The AI sometimes "rounds" for readability. You don't want rounded numbers in a board paper.
2. **The AI misses minority arguments.** If a document has one dissenting view in a footnote, the summary often ignores it. Ask *"what's in the footnotes that isn't in the main text?"* if it matters.
3. **You skip reading entirely.** The summary is for triage and prep, not for replacing the document. If the stakes are high, read the bits the summary flagged as important.

## Chain summaries to save even more time

Got 20 supplier quotes in a folder? Summarise each with the same prompt, paste all 20 summaries into a fresh chat, and ask *"compare these and give me a shortlist of the top 3, with reasons"*. You've just done a full procurement analysis in an afternoon.
