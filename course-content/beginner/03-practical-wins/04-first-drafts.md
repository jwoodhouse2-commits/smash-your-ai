If writing is part of your job, AI is the single biggest time-saver you've got access to. Not because it writes well. Because it writes **fast**, and editing is faster than writing from scratch.

## The mindset shift

Stop thinking of the AI's draft as the finished thing. Start thinking of it as the scaffolding.

Your job is to:

1. Read the AI's first draft.
2. Edit it ruthlessly until it sounds like you, says what you mean, and earns its place.

That's still faster than writing from a blank page. Usually much faster.

## The universal first-draft prompt

Copy this. It works for most "help me write X" tasks.

> You are a writer who mirrors the voice of whoever they're writing for. I need a first draft of [what you're writing]. Target length: [word count or paragraphs].
>
> Context:
> - Audience: [who will read this]
> - Goal: [what I want the reader to do or feel]
> - Tone: [warm / formal / direct / etc.]
> - Things to include: [key points]
> - Things to avoid: [clichés, jargon, specific phrases]
>
> Below are one or two examples of my voice, so you can match it:
>
> [paste 200-500 words of your own writing]
>
> Write the draft. At the end, flag 3 places where I might want to swap in a personal anecdote or a specific example from my experience.

The bit that matters most is **pasting examples of your own voice**. Without it, the AI writes in corporate-default. With it, you get something that actually sounds like you.

## Where AI first drafts work well

- Blog posts and articles
- Email newsletters
- LinkedIn posts
- Sales page copy
- Proposals and briefs
- About pages
- Thank you messages
- Condolence messages (honestly, it's a blessing when you're stuck)
- Job descriptions
- Meeting briefings

## Where AI first drafts don't work well

- **Anything that needs to be funny.** AI humour is almost always bad.
- **Anything very short.** A tweet or a one-line subject is often faster to write than to prompt.
- **Creative fiction with a specific voice.** The AI's defaults drown out anything distinctive.
- **Anything deeply personal or emotional.** It can ghost-write, but the thing you actually feel will be missing.

## The editing habit

Don't just tweak the AI's draft. Do these three things every time.

1. **Rewrite the opening in your own words.** The first 50 words set the voice. AI openings are usually weakest.
2. **Cut at least 30%.** AI over-writes. Your reader will thank you.
3. **Add one specific story or example the AI couldn't know.** A client, a moment, a specific failure or win. That's the bit AI can't fake.

Do that, and readers genuinely won't be able to tell AI helped.
