If you still open Google, skim ten tabs, and try to piece it all together yourself, you're doing research the old way.

The new way: hand the initial digging to AI, and spend your time reading and judging, not searching.

## The setup

Not every AI is good at research. The two that shine are:

- **Gemini.** Google Search is built in. Best for very recent information.
- **ChatGPT** with "Search" toggled on (the paid version does this by default).

Claude can read files and websites you give it, but it doesn't search the web itself unless you add a tool. For pure research, start with Gemini or ChatGPT.

## The research prompt template

> You are a research assistant. I need a brief on [topic].
>
> Specifically, I want to know:
> 1. [question 1]
> 2. [question 2]
> 3. [question 3]
>
> Use the web to find recent sources (prefer the last 12 months where possible). For each claim, cite the source with a link. Flag anything you couldn't find a solid source for. End with 3 questions I should still go and answer myself.

## A real example

**Topic:** "Should a small salon in Northumberland accept Klarna?"

> You are a research assistant. I run a 4-chair hair salon in Northumberland with average ticket £45. I'm considering offering Klarna as a payment option.
>
> Specifically, I want to know:
> 1. What does Klarna cost the merchant per transaction in the UK?
> 2. Do salons typically see higher average spend with Klarna?
> 3. What are the main risks or downsides for a small business?
>
> Use the web to find recent sources (prefer the last 12 months). For each claim, cite the source with a link. Flag anything you couldn't find a solid source for. End with 3 questions I should still answer for myself.

You'll get a tidy brief with real links. Not a novel. Not a fluffy list.

## What to do with the output

Three habits. Do them every time.

1. **Click at least two sources.** If the links don't load, or don't say what the AI claims, that's a hallucination. Don't trust the rest of the brief.
2. **Ignore the summary, read the sources.** The AI's summary is a shortcut. The real value is that you now know which 5 pages to read instead of which 50.
3. **Save the prompt.** Research prompts are eminently reusable. Today Klarna, tomorrow Stripe Terminal, next week GoCardless.

## Where AI research goes wrong

It will still hallucinate. Watch for:

- **Made-up statistics.** *"A 2023 study by XYZ found 73%…"* If you can't find XYZ, cut it.
- **Out-of-date numbers.** Even with search turned on, the AI sometimes cites older articles. Check the dates on sources it quotes.
- **Confident wrongness about UK specifics.** Lots of AI training data is American. For UK tax, VAT, regulation, or pricing, always verify.

## The rule

**AI research is a starting point, not an ending point.**

You're using AI to skip the first 80% of the Google hunt. The last 20% (reading, judging, deciding) is still your job and still the hard part.
