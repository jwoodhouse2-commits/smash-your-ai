**CARE** is the upgrade to RTF for when you know exactly what a good answer looks like, because you've got one in mind (or you've seen one elsewhere).

## The four ingredients

### Context

Everything the AI needs to know about **your world**. Who you are, what the business is, what's happened so far, what you've tried.

This is the biggest section. Don't skimp.

### Action

What you want the AI to do. Specifically. Verb-first.

*"Rewrite this LinkedIn post so it opens with a contrarian hook."*

### Result

What a good answer looks like. This is the subtle bit. You're describing the **shape** of a winning answer, not the answer itself.

*"The ideal post will stop the scroll in the first 10 words, build tension for two sentences, land a concrete example, then finish with a clear reader takeaway."*

### Example

One or two actual examples of what "good" looks like. Could be your own work, could be someone else's. This is where CARE becomes powerful. You stop describing and start **showing**.

## The template

```
Context:
- About me/my business: [detail]
- Situation: [what's happened, what you've tried]
- Audience: [who this is for]

Action: [verb-first instruction: write / rewrite / analyse / produce]

Result I'm looking for:
- [quality 1]
- [quality 2]
- [quality 3]

Example of what good looks like:
[paste 1 or 2 examples]

Now do the task.
```

## A worked example

### The prompt

```
Context:
- I run a 4-chair hair salon in Alnwick, Northumberland.
- I want to write an Instagram caption for a photo of our new
  seasonal autumn colour service.
- Our brand voice is warm, lightly cheeky, never corporate.
- We're a team of three stylists, and we like to mention the
  team by name when we can.

Action: Write an Instagram caption for this service launch.

Result I'm looking for:
- Under 150 characters.
- Opens with something that makes you stop scrolling.
- One light pun or joke.
- Ends with a clear call to book.

Example of what good looks like:
"Autumn on the outside. Glow on the inside. Becky's booking up fast
for our pumpkin-spice tones. Link in bio to snag a chair."

Now write 3 fresh captions in the same style.
```

You get three captions that hit the target. Not three generic AI captions.

## When CARE earns its keep

CARE is overkill for quick asks. But for anything where:

- The tone has to be bang on.
- You've got a specific audience.
- You can point at examples of good work.

it's dramatically better than RTF.

The trick is the **example section**. A single example raises the bar on everything the AI writes after it. Two examples locks the voice in.

## How to find examples

Where to get an example from when you need one:

- **Your own best past work.** Dig through old emails, posts, documents.
- **Competitors whose tone you admire.** Paste in a snippet.
- **Style guides.** If you have one, paste in a chunk.
- **Nothing yet?** Ask the AI to produce 10 different styled drafts, pick your favourite, then use it as the example going forward.

## CARE's weak spot

CARE works less well when the task is exploratory (*"what should I do?"*). For open-ended questions, you don't yet know what a good answer looks like.

For those, [CO-STAR (next lesson)](/course/intermediate/prompting-frameworks/co-star-framework) is the right tool.
