Welcome to the Intermediate tier.

If you've worked through the Beginner tier (or you've been using AI for a few weeks), you'll have noticed something. Some prompts work first time. Others don't. And you can't always explain why.

Frameworks fix that.

## What a framework actually is

A prompting framework is just a **named structure** for a prompt. A mnemonic that reminds you to include the bits that matter: the role, the task, the context, the format, examples, tone.

The ones you'll meet in this module are:

- **[RTF](/course/intermediate/prompting-frameworks/rtf-framework)** (Role, Task, Format). The simplest, the workhorse.
- **[CARE](/course/intermediate/prompting-frameworks/care-framework)** (Context, Action, Result, Example). For when you want specific outcomes.
- **[CO-STAR](/course/intermediate/prompting-frameworks/co-star-framework)** (Context, Objective, Style, Tone, Audience, Response). For when tone and audience really matter.
- **CRISPE** (Capacity, Insight, Statement, Personality, Experiment). For when you need options.

Don't worry about memorising them all. You'll see quickly which ones suit the way your brain works.

## Why they're worth learning

Three reasons.

1. **They give you a checklist.** When a prompt isn't working, the framework tells you what's probably missing. Almost always it's *context* or *audience*.
2. **They speed you up.** Once your brain holds one framework, you write good prompts in 30 seconds instead of three minutes.
3. **They make your prompts portable.** A prompt written with RTF works in ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, or Copilot. It's not tied to one tool.

## When NOT to use a framework

Honestly, a lot of the time.

- Very short tasks (*"Translate this to French"*) don't need scaffolding.
- Ongoing conversations don't need a fresh framework each message.
- Throwaway brainstorms are fine with a one-liner.

Frameworks earn their keep on **important** prompts. The ones where getting a good answer first time matters.

## The meta-point

Frameworks are **training wheels on steroids**. You use them consciously for a while. Eventually you stop noticing them and just write good prompts.

Like learning to drive. You think about every mirror check at week one, and three months later you're doing them without thinking. That's the goal.
