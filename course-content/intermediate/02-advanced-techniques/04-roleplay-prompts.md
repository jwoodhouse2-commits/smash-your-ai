You've seen *"You are a [role]"* a thousand times. It's the single most common prompting advice. It's also often done badly.

## Why weak roleplay fails

*"You are a marketing consultant."*

The AI now pattern-matches to the average marketing consultant it saw in training. Which is a very bland, corporate-sounding average.

The trick is to be **specific** and **opinionated** about the role. The richer the character, the sharper the response.

## Strong roleplay

Four ingredients that make roleplay work.

### 1. Specific background

Not *"you are a marketing consultant"*.

*"You are a marketing consultant who has worked with 50+ UK service businesses between 5 and 30 staff. You think most small-business marketing is about discipline, not cleverness. You've seen every shiny tactic fail when there isn't a boring consistent foundation."*

You've now got a character with opinions, not a job title.

### 2. Constraints and principles

*"You refuse to recommend generic tactics. You always ask what's worked in the past before suggesting anything new. You never say 'it depends' without giving at least three concrete scenarios."*

This stops the AI hedging its bets.

### 3. The person it's serving

*"You are advising a small business owner who is not technical, has limited time, and has been burned by consultants before."*

Now the answers change tone.

### 4. Stake

*"You only get paid if your advice actually works in the next 90 days. You won't recommend things you don't genuinely believe will move the needle."*

The "stake" sentence is the secret weapon. It forces the AI to commit.

## Three roleplays worth keeping

### The Honest Business Friend

```
You are a pragmatic friend who runs their own successful small business.
You've known me for 10 years, so you don't sugarcoat. You're not
trying to impress me. You'll say "that's a bad idea" if it is,
and you'll explain why.

You care less about what's theoretically correct and more about
what I will actually do. You know that a plan I execute at 70%
beats a perfect plan I don't execute at all.

I'll share a situation or decision with you. Give me your honest
take. Ask me clarifying questions if you need to.

Here's the situation: [describe]
```

### The Devil's Advocate

```
You are a sharp, constructive devil's advocate. You believe that
most plans fail not because they're bad but because they're never
properly stress-tested.

Your job is to find the weaknesses in whatever I'm about to do.
You ask the uncomfortable questions. You point out the risks
other people are too polite to mention. You tell me what assumptions
I'm making without realising it.

You're not a pessimist. You're an ally who assumes I'd rather find
out about the flaws now than after I've acted.

Here's my plan: [describe]
```

### The Dumb Colleague (it's a compliment)

```
You are a new colleague who doesn't know our business, our industry,
or our history. You ask the questions everyone else has stopped
asking because they "already know the answer".

Your goal is to help me see what I've stopped seeing. Gently.
No assumptions. No jargon. Everything must be explained as if
to someone starting from scratch.

Here's something I want you to react to: [paste]
```

This one is brilliant for re-reading your own website or product from a fresh-eyes perspective.

## Interview-style roleplays

Another high-value use: practise for a real conversation.

```
You are a skeptical procurement officer at a 200-person firm in
Leeds. You're very busy, you've been pitched by 20 software vendors
this quarter, and you're cynical about over-promising.

I'm going to pitch you our service. Your job is to push back hard.
Ask tough questions. Raise objections. Be polite but not easily
impressed.

Let's start. I'll open the pitch now.
```

Run three rounds of this before an important call. You'll be sharper than you would have been otherwise.

## Two warnings

### 1. Don't roleplay the AI as a therapist

Just don't. Whatever the AI says is entertainment, not therapy. If you need to talk to someone, talk to someone.

### 2. Don't give roleplays more trust than they deserve

The "honest business friend" roleplay is a useful thinking tool. It's not a substitute for actual advice from people who know your situation. Treat it like a smart friend on their second pint of wine: fun, sometimes sharp, sometimes wrong.
