Of all the AI tools Google has launched, **NotebookLM** is the one that most deserves to be better known. If you do any kind of research (reading papers, studying a topic, analysing past meeting notes, learning a new skill), this is your new best friend.

And it's **free**.

## What NotebookLM actually is

You upload up to 50 sources per notebook. PDFs, Google Docs, web pages, YouTube videos, pasted text, audio recordings.

Then you ask questions. NotebookLM answers **only using the sources you uploaded**, and cites the exact passage it got the answer from.

In practice: no hallucinations (within reason), no "here's some generic info from the web", and no guessing. Just answers from your actual material.

## Where to find it

Go to [notebooklm.google.com](https://notebooklm.google.com) and sign in with a Google account. That's it. Free to use.

## Three real-world uses

### 1. Studying for something specific

Upload the 3-5 most authoritative articles on a topic. Ask questions. NotebookLM turns the whole set into a searchable, interactive textbook.

Examples:

- Preparing for a board meeting on a new regulation: upload the regulation, the industry commentary, and any relevant news articles.
- Choosing between two software options: upload each vendor's documentation, their pricing page, and two comparison articles.
- Researching a new market: upload a market report, three relevant company filings, a competitor's investor deck.

You can then ask:

> *"What's the difference between option A and option B on pricing?"*
> 
> *"What do the sources disagree on?"*
> 
> *"Summarise the three things I most need to understand before the meeting."*

### 2. Turning your own content into a searchable archive

Upload all your past blog posts, internal documents, training materials, or client proposals. Now ask:

> *"Have we written about X before?"*
> 
> *"What's our position on Y?"*
> 
> *"Give me a one-paragraph version of our pitch, using our own past wording."*

For anyone who's been writing for years and has lost track of what's where, this is magic.

### 3. Extracting insights from meetings

Record meetings (with permission). Auto-generate transcripts. Upload multiple months' worth to a notebook. Then:

> *"What's the pattern of concerns clients have raised about [X] over the last 6 months?"*
> 
> *"When did we first discuss changing our pricing?"*
> 
> *"List every commitment the team has made but not delivered on."*

## NotebookLM's quirks

### It's stronger than ChatGPT on accuracy

Because NotebookLM only answers from your sources, hallucinations are dramatically rarer. If the answer isn't in the sources, it'll say so.

### It's weaker than ChatGPT on creativity

Ask NotebookLM to write you a poem or brainstorm 20 ideas and it'll be polite but uninspired. This isn't its job.

### It's good at two things the chat AIs aren't

- **Audio overviews.** Turn your notebook into a two-host podcast-style discussion. Useful for commuting-time study or sharing with a team.
- **Study guides.** Click "Study guide" and it auto-generates quiz questions, flashcards, and key concepts from your sources.

## The workflow that changes things

Here's the workflow that made me a true NotebookLM fan.

1. When researching anything non-trivial, start a **new notebook** for that topic.
2. Over the next few days, **keep adding sources** as you find them. Articles, meeting notes, competitor pages, internal docs.
3. Every time a question comes up, **ask the notebook first**, not Google.
4. Use the notebook as your thinking space. Ask it to summarise. Compare sources. Find contradictions. Challenge you.

It becomes a memory-extension for a project. Three months in, you can ask "what did we decide about X" and it finds the answer even when you can't remember what document it was in.
