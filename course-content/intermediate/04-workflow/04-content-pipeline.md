Content is where AI most changes the maths for a small business. The bottleneck used to be writing. Now it's **choosing what to write about**.

Here's a 5-step pipeline that works. It's boring and unglamorous, and that's why it works.

## Step 1: Idea generation

Weekly, not daily.

Open your preferred chat AI. Run this:

```
You are my content strategist. My business is [DESCRIPTION] and
my audience is [AUDIENCE].

Generate 20 content ideas for the next month. Mix:
- 5 "here's how" educational posts.
- 5 personal or behind-the-scenes moments.
- 5 contrarian or opinion posts.
- 5 newsjackable angles (things happening in our industry now).

Each idea should come with a one-line logline and a suggested
platform (blog, LinkedIn, Instagram, newsletter).

After the list, flag your top 5 favourites and say why.
```

Pick 3-5 ideas to actually write. Put them on your content calendar.

## Step 2: Outline

For each picked idea, outline before drafting.

```
Take this idea: [IDEA + LOGLINE].

Write a 4-part outline for a [BLOG POST / LINKEDIN POST / NEWSLETTER].

Structure:
1. Hook (the angle that makes this different from every other
   piece on this topic).
2. The core idea, in 2-3 sentences.
3. The "proof" - either a specific example, a contrarian point,
   or a stat.
4. The takeaway (what should the reader do or think differently).

Keep it punchy. Flag 2 questions I should answer from my own
experience before drafting.
```

Those "questions from my own experience" are the secret. They're what make your post yours.

## Step 3: First draft

With the outline approved, draft.

```
Using the outline above, write a first draft. [WORD COUNT].

Voice rules:
- British English.
- No em dashes.
- Contractions are fine.
- Short sentences.
- One concrete example per section.

Here are 3 examples of my writing voice:
[PASTE]

Draft the post. At the end, flag 3 places where I should swap
in a real story from my experience.
```

## Step 4: Human pass

Don't skip this.

Read the draft. Do the following manually:

1. **Rewrite the opening line in your own words.** First 50 words set the voice. AI openings are almost always weakest.
2. **Cut 20-30%.** AI draft always over-writes. Your reader will thank you.
3. **Add the specific story the AI flagged.** A real client, a real moment, a real failure or win. That's the bit the AI can't fake.

This is the step where the post becomes yours.

## Step 5: Distribution pack

One more AI pass, but focused on distribution.

```
Given the final blog post below, generate:
- 3 LinkedIn post variants (each 150-220 words, each pulling out
  a different angle from the blog).
- 5 tweet/X variants (each under 280 chars).
- 1 newsletter intro paragraph that links to the full post.
- 2 email subject lines.

Use the same voice as the blog. No em dashes. British English.

[paste final blog post]
```

In two minutes you've got a week of distribution content. Schedule in your usual tool.

## The full cadence

Once this pipeline is in place, the weekly rhythm is:

- **Monday morning:** step 1 (ideas). 15 minutes.
- **Tuesday-Wednesday:** step 2 and 3 for the week's post. 45 minutes.
- **Thursday:** step 4 (human pass). 30 minutes.
- **Friday:** step 5 (distribution pack). 10 minutes.

Total: 1 hour 40 minutes for one high-quality piece of content plus a full week of social distribution.

Without AI, the same output probably takes 4-5 hours.

## Three mistakes to avoid

### 1. Letting the AI write it all

If you skip step 4, your content ends up sounding generic. The compound effect is that **your voice disappears from your own content**. Over 6 months, your audience will notice before you do.

### 2. Over-engineering

You don't need a custom GPT for each step. A saved prompt template in your notes app is fine. Simpler = more likely to actually use it.

### 3. Chasing volume over resonance

AI makes it trivial to post every day. That doesn't mean you should. One really good post a week beats five mediocre ones. AI should free you up to write things that are **worth** publishing, not to publish everything.
