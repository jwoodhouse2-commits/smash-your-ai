This course has spent a lot of lessons on what Claude Code can do. The final lesson is about the jobs it **should not** do, and why.

Being clear-eyed about this is the difference between an AI power user and an AI user who eventually gets burned.

## Six times to close the terminal

### 1. When you do not know what good looks like

If you cannot recognise a correct answer, you cannot review the AI's answer. You are not saving time; you are outsourcing your judgement to something that does not actually have any.

**Examples:** legal advice if you are not a lawyer; medical advice; investment decisions; contracts where you do not know what clauses matter.

**Do instead:** spend the time on learning the basics, or hire a human who knows.

### 2. When the stakes are high and errors are permanent

Agents make mistakes. Most of them are recoverable. Some are not.

**Examples:** sending the email, posting the tweet, deploying to production, pressing "submit" on a tax return, deleting anything irreplaceable.

**Do instead:** use Claude Code up to the point of "draft is ready". Do the final send, post, or submit manually. The two-second pause of clicking the button yourself has saved a lot of people a bad week.

### 3. When the relationship matters more than the output

Some jobs are outputs. Some jobs are relationships. If you are writing a difficult message to someone you care about, the medium is the message. Making it efficient kills the thing that makes it valuable.

**Examples:** a personal apology; a condolence note; your vows; a sales call with your most important client; a tough performance conversation with a colleague.

**Do instead:** write it yourself. Use AI for the admin around it, not for the thing itself.

### 4. When you are the expert

If the task is in your zone of expertise, you are usually faster and better than the AI. AI is at its best as a force multiplier on things you are not expert in.

**Examples:** you have written 400 of these emails; you have taught this lesson 50 times; you know the answer to the question and are just looking for someone to type it.

**Do instead:** use AI for the peripheral work (editing, formatting, filing). Do the expert bit yourself. You will be quicker, and it will be better.

### 5. When learning is the goal

If the point of the task was for you to learn how to do it, letting the AI do it skips the learning.

**Examples:** a language you are trying to learn; a skill you are trying to build; a topic you are trying to understand deeply.

**Do instead:** use AI as a tutor, not a ghostwriter. "Check my understanding, don't do the thinking for me" is a great prompt.

### 6. When the task is small

Claude Code has a fixed overhead. Opening the terminal, writing the prompt, waiting, reading the output, approving. If the whole task would have taken 90 seconds, that overhead is most of your elapsed time.

**Examples:** a one-line typo fix; renaming a single file; looking up a number you already half-remember.

**Do instead:** just do it.

## The one rule

If you cannot articulate **why you are using Claude Code for this specific task**, you probably should not be.

The users who get the most from AI are not the ones who use it the most. They are the ones who pick their spots.

- Expert at repetitive admin? Automate it.
- Weak at writing first drafts? Use it to start.
- Strong at judgement and editing? Do that part yourself.
- Stuck on something you do not understand? Use AI as a tutor.

The worst users are the ones who put everything through AI without asking why.

    <blockquote>"AI for the repetitive stuff, my brain for the decisions, my heart for the people."</blockquote>

    <p>Stick the list on the wall near your computer. Every user who has done this says the same thing afterwards: their use of AI got better because they stopped using it indiscriminately.</p>

## You are at the end of the course

If you have been ticking off lessons to get here, congratulations. You have gone from "what is AI?" all the way to Claude Code, MCP, and agents. The frontier is a fast-moving place, and the course will add more lessons as the tools change.

The single biggest thing to remember:

**You are not a better professional because you use AI. You are a better professional because you make good decisions about when, how, and why.**

Claude Code, and every agent like it, is a tool. Powerful tools amplify whatever you bring to them. If what you bring is judgement and care, they make you formidable. If what you bring is volume without thinking, they make you louder.

Pick your spots. Review the output. Keep your hands on the wheel for anything that matters.

Good luck.
